## 2. Investment Thesis

Multi-strategy hedge funds allocate capital across strategy return streams whose risk contributions change with market regimes. The core problem is **dynamic risk budgeting**: when one sleeve’s volatility spikes, capital should shrink there and rotate toward more stable sleeves — without abandoning diversification.

This study treats publicly traded ETFs as transparent proxies for momentum, defensive, and crisis strategies, then compares naive inverse-vol risk parity against covariance-aware minimum variance and max diversification, all versus static benchmarks.

## 4. Methodology

- **Risk window:** trailing 60 trading days (vol + covariance); no lookahead  
- **InvVol:** \(w_i \propto 1/\sigma_i\), long-only, sum to 100%  
- **MinVar:** minimize \(w'\Sigma w\), bounds 5%–60% per sleeve  
- **MaxDiv:** maximize diversification ratio under same bounds  
- **Friction:** 10bps per unit one-way turnover (dynamic methods only)  
- **Risk-free rate:** 4.5% for Sharpe / Sortino

## 5. Results

InvVol cut max drawdown versus equal-weight and lifted Sharpe from −0.50 to 0.30. MinVar was more conservative on volatility but lagged InvVol after costs. **60/40 won on absolute return because it holds zero VIXY** — that is the economic story, not a coding artifact.

## 6. Regime Analysis

- **COVID crash:** Equal-weight’s VIXY sleeve produced large positive period returns (crisis convexity); 60/40 sold off without a vol hedge.  
- **2022 rate shock:** Grinding rates-driven bear — crisis sleeve helped less than in March 2020.  
- **Calm / recovery years:** Paying permanent VIXY carry hurts relative performance.

## 7. Risks & Limitations

1. VIXY roll/contango decay — poor permanent hedge  
2. ETF proxies ≠ live hedge-fund P&L  
3. Daily rebalancing overstates institutional turnover  
4. Long-only, no leverage, no cash sleeve  
5. Past regimes are not forecasts  

---

## 8. Conclusion

For a **risk-conscious** allocator who wants a dedicated crisis sleeve, inverse-volatility risk parity improves Sharpe and drawdowns versus naive equal-weight after realistic friction.

For a **return-seeking** mandate that can live without permanent vol exposure, static 60/40 dominates on CAGR because it avoids VIXY decay.

The professional takeaway: the optimal allocator depends on whether the investment committee values crisis convexity enough to pay its carry cost.
